Case 1 • Google Pay

Google Pay Gifting Vouchers

A gifting layer on top of UPI with two products: a small-value Money Voucher and a brand-agnostic Gift Voucher. The cultural anchor is the Indian moi or shagun envelope: money given at a function with a personal touch.

Problem

  • UPI is excellent for payments but flat for gifting: Sending ₹501 for Pongal feels the same as paying a bill, so the emotional moment is missing.
  • Digital gift cards lock users to one brand: Amazon, Swiggy, and similar cards force a specific merchant, so the receiver may not want what the sender chose.
  • Unclaimed money anxiety: Senders worry about money that is never claimed or never used.

Users

  • Sender: Working adult sending festival, birthday, or function money to family and friends.
  • Receiver: Family member, often older and less app-savvy. Each voucher is a chance to onboard them.
  • Merchant: A local kirana, sweet shop, or textile shop that already shows a UPI QR.

Solution

Money Voucher (small, cash-like)

Capped amount (start at ₹5,000 per voucher; tune the monthly cap in a pilot). Sent as a scratch card with a greeting message. The receiver reveals the amount, then claims it. If unclaimed in 7 days, the amount refunds automatically to the sender's bank account.

Gift Voucher (choice, not cash)

Redeemable across partner brands (shopping, food delivery, OTT) through a "Pay with Gift Voucher" option at checkout. A reminder at day 90: if still unused, the full balance rolls into Google Play balance with no loss of value.

Local Merchant Redemption

Vouchers can be spent at verified merchants by scanning their QR. Merchants show a "GPay Voucher Accepted" sticker.

Family Circle

Family members scan each other's QR to join a circle and earn points. Points convert into free Gift Voucher credit for the next celebration.

Key Decisions and Trade-offs

1
Small cap: Keeps the product gifting-sized rather than a transfer tool and limits fraud exposure. Cost: large gifts are excluded.
2
Verified accounts only: Sender, receiver, and merchant must all be KYC-verified. Cost: friction at claim. Mitigation: light KYC at claim, full KYC later.
3
Merchant payments only (P2M): Voucher balance cannot be sent to a person's QR, which closes the cash-out loophole where a friend scans a personal QR to turn a voucher into cash.
4
Gate on business verification, not account type: Many small shops use savings-account QRs, so requiring a current account would miss the target merchants.
5
Rollover framed as value kept: Revenue comes from the user later spending Play balance, not from keeping the unused amount.
North-Star Metric
Activated receivers per 100 vouchers sent
Measures new receiver onboarding and network adoption.
Secondary Metrics
Claim rate within 7 days; auto-refund rate; merchant sticker display rate; redemptions per merchant.
Guardrails
Cash-out attempt rate; complaint rate; KYC drop-off at claim.

Business Impact (Hypotheses)

  • Brand partners fund a fee of roughly 0.5% to 1% on Gift Voucher redemption.
  • Google earns standard Play commission when rolled-over balance is spent.
  • Festival-season engagement spikes and a built-in receiver onboarding loop.

Rollout and Validation

  • Step 1: Pilot Money Voucher only, in one state, around one festival.
  • Step 2: Add Gift Voucher with 5 to 10 brands.
  • Step 3: Add verified-merchant redemption and Family Circle.
Case 2 • Search and Gemini

Google Search Campus Guild

A micro-annotation programme where college students review and correct Search and Gemini answers in any language, using their own slang, and earn recognition instead of cash.

Problem

  • AI misses regional context and slang: AI answers often miss real-world context, regional nuance, and code-mixed language in every Indian language, such as Tanglish (Tamil mixed with English) or Hinglish (Hindi mixed with English).
  • Students create slang, raters do not: The people who create current slang are students, but most formal review work is done in standardized language.
  • Slow and expensive collection: Quality data in these languages is expensive and slow to collect through traditional agencies.

Users & Differentiator

  • Contributors: College and school students fluent in any language, including regional and code-mixed ones.
  • Colleges: Want credible, skill-building activities for students.
  • Google teams: Need high-quality evaluation and training data for regional queries.
  • Differentiator: Differs from generic rating programmes by using the campus channel and focusing specifically on code-mixed regional slang.

Solution

Daily Micro-Tasks

Each student reviews 10 to 15 queries a day and judges whether the answer matches real intent, correcting where it does not.

Consensus Check

3 to 5 students rate the same query. Only agreed answers count towards evaluation datasets.

Gold Questions

Items with known answers are mixed in to catch careless or spam submissions.

Incentives Without Cash

An official contributor certificate at milestones, college-level points and leaderboards, and fast-track consideration for internships.

Privacy by Design

Students see anonymised sample queries only, never real user personal data.

Key Decisions and Trade-offs

1
Certificate instead of pay: Cheap to run and good for résumés. Risk: unpaid-labour criticism and weak retention. Mitigation: opt-in, clear value exchange, and a pilot test of stipends if retention is low.
2
Campus-first distribution: Colleges provide trust and reach. Cost: academic-calendar seasonality.
3
Language scope: Open to every language from day one, with students choosing the languages they know. Quality is checked per language.
North-Star Metric
Agreement-checked corrections per active student per week
Ensures both throughput volume and multi-evaluator consensus.
Secondary Metrics
Annotator agreement rate; share of items passing gold questions; active students per college; 30-day retention.
Guardrails
Privacy incidents; student workload hours; spam rate.

Business Impact (Hypotheses)

  • Free or low-cost, high-quality regional training and evaluation data.
  • Better AI understanding of local queries across Search and Gemini.
  • A pre-vetted talent pipeline for Google internships.

Rollout and Validation

  • Step 1: Pilot with 5 colleges in one state for one term, covering the languages those students use.
  • Step 2: Compare corrected-data impact against a baseline test set.
  • Step 3: Decide on stipends, new states, and college partnerships based on retention.
Case 3 • YouTube Shorts

YouTube Shorts Smart Bar

A redesign of the Shorts viewing screen around three ideas: a clean screen with one hidden options sheet, a Products drawer for commerce, and a top bar the viewer arranges themselves.

Interactive Companion: Shorts App Flow

A full 7-screen interactive app prototype detailing the Smart Bar, double-tap heart, and Products drawer.

View 7-Screen App Flow →

Problem

  • Cluttered player: The player is cluttered compared with competing short-video apps, with many icons competing for attention.
  • Buried clear screen: The existing menu already contains a "Clear Screen" option, which suggests clutter is acknowledged but the fix is buried.
  • Weak monetization: Heavy viewership has not translated into strong ad revenue per view.
  • Brand safety: Shady or adult ads push away family audiences.
  • Rigid feed: The feed is hard to steer toward what a viewer wants.

Users

  • Viewers: Browse one-handed on a phone and want an unobstructed video canvas.
  • Creators: Want to earn from products they already show in videos.
  • Brands: Want safe, high-intent placements without adult ad adjacency.

Solution

1. Smart Bar (clean UI)

Bottom bar: Home, Comments, Create (+), Search, and an arrow. All main actions sit in the thumb zone. The arrow opens a white sheet with Save, Description, Captions, Quality, Not interested, Hide channel, Report, and Feedback. Tapping the video closes it. Remix is merged into Create because both capture video. Save and Watchlist become one Save (Watch later, with an option to choose a playlist). Share stays visible in the title row because sharing drives growth. The profile moves to top right.

2. Like Interaction

Double-tap likes. The heart turns from outline to red, beats for a few seconds, and sends hearts floating up the right edge. It resets on the next Short.

3. Products Drawer

A Products chip appears under the title only when items are tagged or detected. Creator-tagged items earn higher affiliate commission. Untagged items can be matched by visual recognition and linked to partner shopping apps.

4. Personal Top Bar

Default chips (for example Food, Tech) plus a plus button. Viewers add states or interests such as Tamil Nadu and arrange them in their own order.

Brand Safety (Supporting)

Stricter moderation on adult or shady ads and a one-tap Block Ad option. Keep ad load balanced against revenue per view.

Key Decisions and Trade-offs

1
Hide options, keep core ones visible: Cost: lower discovery of hidden options. Mitigation: a first-time hint and a clear arrow.
2
Share visible, Save inside sheet: Protects the viral loop while decluttering.
3
Products chip only when relevant: Avoids adding clutter to every video.
4
User-arranged chips rather than fixed filters: Gives control without a heavy settings screen.
North-Star Metric
Watch time per session without a drop in share rate
Ensures immersion increases while organic sharing stays healthy.
Secondary Metrics
Share rate and save rate vs today; arrow open rate; Products chip tap rate; click-outs to partner apps.
Guardrails
Accidental-tap rate; ad complaint rate; creator earnings.

Business Impact (Hypotheses)

  • Affiliate and referral revenue from shopping partners.
  • Shopping-intent signals for ad targeting.
  • Premium advertisers attracted by better brand safety.
  • Longer, more satisfied sessions from a cleaner screen and steerable feed.

Rollout and Validation

  • Step 1: A/B test the Smart Bar layout with a small share of users.
  • Step 2: Pilot the Products drawer with a few creators and shopping partners.
  • Step 3: Test the personal top bar in one state.
Case 4 • Google Meet

Google Meet Couple Pack

An OTT-branded Couple Pack, powered by Google Meet, that lets two people in different places watch the same show in sync with a simple video bubble.

Problem

  • Screen share breaks on protected content: Long-distance couples and friends who share a screen on Meet hit a black screen because of DRM protection, and audio drifts out of sync.
  • Third-party tools are fragile: Third-party watch-party tools need extensions, are unofficial, and work poorly on TV and phone.
  • Low non-work Meet usage: Meet has no official co-watching mode, so evening and weekend consumer use stays low.

Users & Partners

  • Couples & friends in different cities: In India this includes migrant workers and students away from home.
  • OTT platforms: Want more subscribers and lower churn.
  • Google Meet: Wants more non-work daily active usage.

Solution

Official Partner Integration

Playback is synced by the OTT's own player, so there is no screen share, no black screen, and no lag.

Couple Pack

Sold by the OTT as a two-person plan (two linked profiles for long-distance pairs), with Meet as the co-watch layer.

Round Video Bubble & Audio Ducking

The show plays full screen while the partner appears in a draggable round bubble. Audio ducks automatically when either person speaks.

Invite Flow

One person starts a session and the other joins through a single link.

Key Decisions and Trade-offs

1
Partner-led instead of Google charging per movie: Removes the hard sell of an extra fee on top of a subscription. Cost: revenue depends on OTT agreements.
2
Start with mid-size Indian OTTs: Mid-size platforms are hungry for subscribers, then approach larger platforms.
3
Pricing as a test: Price points are decided in the pilot, not fixed now.
4
UX first: The bubble and audio ducking are the strongest differentiator and can ship even before pricing is final.
North-Star Metric
Active couple pairs per month
Reflects ongoing pair adoption and retention.
Secondary Metrics
Sessions per pair per month; average session length; churn of paired accounts vs solo accounts; conversion of the second profile.
Guardrails
Sync failure rate; support tickets; partner satisfaction.

Why the OTT Says Yes

  • A second paying subscriber from the same household of interest.
  • Lower churn, because couples who watch together stay subscribed longer.
  • Serves a long-distance segment that is not served well today.

Rollout and Validation

  • Step 1: Pilot with one OTT on one corridor (for example Chennai and Bangalore) during a festival week.
  • Step 2: Measure pair activation, sessions, and churn against a control group.
  • Step 3: Use the results to approach larger OTT platforms.

Why These Four Matter for Google

Together the four cases cover growth, data quality, monetization, and engagement, and each can be tested with a small pilot before large investment.

Case What Google Gains Main Lever
1. Gifting Vouchers New activated receivers, festival-season engagement, a wider verified-merchant footprint, Play spend commission Payments growth
2. Campus Guild Low-cost multilingual evaluation data, better Search and Gemini answers for Indian users, an internship talent pipeline Product quality and talent
3. Shorts Smart Bar Commerce revenue, longer and cleaner sessions, safer inventory for premium advertisers Monetization
4. Couple Pack Non-work Meet usage, recurring revenue share with OTT partners Engagement and partnerships

India-First Insight

Festivals and moi culture, regional languages, local merchants, migrant families.

Built-in Growth Loops

A voucher onboards the receiver, a contributor brings classmates, a creator tag feeds commerce, a pair needs two accounts.

Trust by Design

Verified accounts and caps, anonymised data, brand-safety controls, partner-led integrations.

Small First Steps

Every case ends with a pilot small enough for one team to run before scaling.

Suggested Sequence

  1. Shorts Smart Bar and Gifting Vouchers first: Closest to revenue and engagement, and Shorts changes can be A/B tested quickly.
  2. Campus Guild next: A low-cost pilot with a few colleges.
  3. Couple Pack last: Depends on OTT partner agreements.
